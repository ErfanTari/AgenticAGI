    # Comprehensive Ceramic Glaze Testing Procedure

    ## 1. Materials Needed
    - **Glazes:** Raw glaze materials, commercial glazes, test samples.
    - **Clay Bodies:** Bisque-fired tiles (3x3 inches or larger), matching clay to intended use.
    - **Tools:** Sieves (60-80 mesh), mixing buckets, stirrers, dip sticks, spray gun, brushes.
    - **Equipment:** Kiln with temperature controller, thermocouples, pyrometers.
    - **Consumables:** Water, masking tape, permanent markers for labeling.

    ## 2. Safety Precautions
    - **Respiratory Protection:** Wear a NIOSH-approved respirator (N95 or P100) when mixing dry glaze materials to prevent silica dust inhalation.
    - **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE):** Use safety goggles, nitrile gloves, and aprons during application.
    - **Ventilation:** Ensure adequate ventilation in the mixing area; use a spray booth with filtration for spraying applications.
    - **Toxic Materials:** Exercise extreme caution with lead, cadmium, barium, or manganese compounds. Follow SDS guidelines strictly.
    - **Kiln Safety:** Allow kilns to cool completely before opening to avoid thermal shock and burns.

    ## 3. Test Tile Preparation
    - **Selection:** Choose clay bodies that match the intended final product (e.g., stoneware, porcelain).
    - **Forming:** Create uniform tiles approximately 3x3 inches with a consistent thickness of 1/4 to 1/2 inch.
    - **Drying:** Allow tiles to dry completely to prevent warping or cracking during bisque firing.
    - **Bisque Firing:** Fire clay bodies to maturity (e.g., Cone 06-08) before glazing.
    - **Labeling:** Mark the back of each tile with a unique identifier, date, and intended glaze code using a permanent marker or pencil.

    ## 4. Application Methods

    ### Brushing
    - Stir glaze thoroughly to ensure even suspension of particles.
    - Apply thin, even coats using soft bristle brushes.
    - Allow each coat to dry slightly before applying the next (typically 2-3 coats for opacity).
    - Avoid pooling at edges; wipe the bottom edge clean to prevent sticking to the kiln shelf.

    ### Dipping
    - Adjust glaze viscosity to a creamy consistency suitable for dipping.
    - Submerge tile completely into the bucket, then remove smoothly.
    - Control immersion time based on desired thickness (typically 2-5 seconds).
    - Remove excess glaze by tapping the tile gently against the rim of the container.

    ### Spraying
    - Use a compressed air spray gun with appropriate nozzle size.
    - Maintain consistent distance and speed while moving across the tile surface.
    - Apply multiple light coats to prevent running or uneven coverage.
    - Ensure proper airflow in the spray booth to capture overspray.

    ## 5. Firing Schedules
    - **Preparation:** Load kiln with adequate spacing between tiles for air circulation.
    - **Ramp Rate:** Heat at a controlled rate (e.g., 100-200°F per hour) during the quartz inversion phase to prevent thermal shock.
    - **Soak:** Hold at peak temperature for 5-10 minutes to ensure even maturation and glaze flow.
    - **Temperature Range:** Record exact cone or Celsius/Fahrenheit target (e.g., Cone 6, 2232°F).

    ## 6. Cooling Procedures
    - **Natural Cooling:** Allow the kiln to cool naturally without forced air until below 1000°F.
    - **Annealing Zone:** Slow cooling through the critical annealing range (approx. 1400°F-900°F) prevents cracking due to thermal stress.
    - **Unloading:** Open kiln only when interior temperature is safe for handling (below 200°F).
    - **Post-Fire Inspection:** Inspect tiles immediately after removal while warm but cool enough to handle safely.

    ## 7. Evaluation Criteria

    ### Visual and Physical Properties
    - **Color:** Assess under natural daylight and artificial light; note variations from expected results.
    - **Texture:** Evaluate surface feel (smooth, rough, matte, glossy) and visual texture (crystalline, pitted).
    - **Adhesion:** Perform a scratch test with a metal tool or key to ensure glaze is fused properly to the body.

    ### Defect Analysis
    - **Crazing:** Check for fine surface cracks in the glaze indicating tensile stress; indicates glaze is too tight compared to clay.
    - **Shivering:** Look for compression cracks or chips at edges, indicating glaze is under tension.
    - **Crawling:** Identify areas where glaze has retracted leaving bare clay spots due to poor adhesion or trapped air.
    - **Pinholes/Blisters:** Inspect surface for small holes or raised bumps caused by gas release during firing.

    ## 8. Documentation Requirements
    - **Glaze Log:** Record exact recipe weights, batch numbers, and water-to-glaze ratios.
    - **Firing Data:** Document kiln type, element condition, thermocouple readings, and specific ramp/soak times used.
    - **Photographic Evidence:** Take high-resolution photos of each tile under consistent lighting conditions for future comparison.
    - **Results Summary:** Note pass/fail status against functional requirements (e.g., food safe, thermal shock resistance).
    - **Storage:** Store physical test tiles
